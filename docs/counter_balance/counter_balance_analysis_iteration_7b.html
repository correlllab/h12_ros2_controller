Test the mechanism, not the policy label.

The directional sentinel improves again, but every overhang pair in this panel has more task-added movement. The return-command weakness repeats across both sides. Neither result calls for a FAME-specific trick.

12 new hardware recordings, 4,057 samples, six explicit pairs. The new ALMI checkpoint has not been tested; cross-policy effectiveness is pending its own matched baseline. No hardware was operated by this investigation.

Decision

Keep the current hardware settings frozen for the upcoming ALMI comparison. Investigate a generic bounded postfade recovery reference offline, while treating movement/fade deterioration as a separate objective/response problem. Reduced arm-only or full horizontal model momentum is not enough to select a controller.
The current unchanged full-body simulator failed before movement. No whole-body simulated recovery improvement is established and no candidate is cleared for hardware. Offline command-law and actuator tests are a narrower evidence source, not a substitute for that comparison.

No pre-onset extrapolation or arbitrary sample exclusion. Common-window/100 ms sensitivities preserve movement tilt/onset/gravity signs. Different preparation offsets, no independently attested checkpoint ID, sparse repetition and recorder-version changes limit causal/generalization claims. FAME's intentional zero-input setup was not altered.

Three separate questions

Movement

Does the requested and realized counter action reduce full modeled angular momentum/rate and observed base motion, or merely the selected arm-only residual? Keep support/contact assumptions explicit.

Fade

Does the change in balancing weight create a rate transient or inherit a disturbance already growing during motion? A peak during fade alone is not proof of its cause.

Postfade return

Does a measured-recentered one-step command leave a repeatable load-dependent return offset? Better joint return must also preserve base recovery, endpoint tracking and effort margins.

Commands are active; return remains weak

All 2,029 Counter samples independently reproduce the recorded bounded least-squares optimum. There are no solver retries, backtracks or invalid candidates. Local buffer velocity equals applied velocity exactly; counter position obeys q_cmd=q_captured+u/30 to within 2.98×10−8 rad. The controller's captured state is not always the earlier recorder snapshot; joint positions can differ by 0.00728 rad. This establishes command intent, not per-command motor acknowledgement.

Sideways motion binds a rate or excursion constraint in 23/36, 24/37 and 23/37 samples; inner-forward binds none. Measured speed/excursion can exceed command envelopes: elbow speed reaches 1.108 rad/s and measured excursion 0.5953 rad versus nominal bounds 1 rad/s and 0.58 rad. Raising authority is not the first response to these observations.

Do not tune momentum-rate control from a fragile derivative

All recorded joint ddq values are zero; none were treated as measured acceleration. World-frame CAM was differentiated on 30 Hz grids with 3-, 5- and 9-sample quadratic filters, including the rotating-frame transport term. F01's whole-body horizontal rate RMS changes from Frame/Counter 3.919/4.160 Nm to 1.904/2.062 Nm to 1.134/0.947 Nm as the filter grows: the apparent sign of improvement reverses.

Quaternion-derived and gyro-derived rates also disagree at roughly 0.014–0.017 rad/s RMS. A quaternion-root alternative reverses F04r's small horizontal-CAM difference. These sensitivities prevent a defensible “momentum-rate constraint will fix this” conclusion now. Validate the signal/model consistency first; do not smooth until the preferred answer appears.

Bounded offline experiments

One generic candidate: after T+0.5 s only, initialize a persistent counter position reference from the last accepted command and slew toward the captured posture. Keep all four joints, the movement/fade path, moving-arm targets, policy inputs and physics fixed. No target-ID or FAME-specific branch changes the law.

Before testing, the screening rule required at least 25% and 0.02 rad reduction in late elbow error on each mirrored case, with no material body/endpoint/effort regressions. Provisional physical guards were terminal drift no worse than baseline +max(0.1°,10%), postfade gyro +max(0.01 rad/s,10%), endpoint error +max(1 mm,10%), and no new rejection or constraint violation. These are offline engineering screens, not hardware safety tolerances or statistical bounds.

1. Full-body baseline: failed before motion

One of at most four planned full simulator cells was used: unchanged FAME left sideways, real-aligned upper profile, frozen policy/encoder, 0.8x and isolated domain 91. Standing initialization failed after about 19.6 s, with base height 0.0496 m and tilt 2.330 rad; task release never occurred. An outer fell:false field must not be read as upright success. The remaining full cells were stopped according to the predeclared gate.

No physics, policy input, limit or readiness threshold was tuned to make it pass. The simulation initialization/observation sequence must first reproduce a valid unchanged baseline before it can rank a full-body controller change. That is a separate baseline issue, not evidence that the recovery candidate causes a fall.

2. Recorded-state replay: command behavior, not physical recovery

Three recordings were replayed through actual deployed candidate validation, collision checks, command writing and float32 clipping with an in-memory publisher. Sensor trajectories were held fixed. All 1,013 baseline position-buffer rows reproduce exactly; pre-transition and moving-arm position buffers remain unchanged.

Every frozen-state replay eventually reaches the 0.04 rad lead guard, rejects the proposal and latches measured-position hold. This is expected when an alternative reference is tested against a sensor trajectory that cannot respond to it. It verifies that the reference does not accumulate indefinitely; it does not demonstrate that the real arm would recover.

The temporary guard settings were the same across all four joints/cases: desired recovery speed 0.20 rad/s, normal acceleration limit 1 rad/s², lead 0.04 rad, and estimated total-effort caps [24,24,10.8,10.8] Nm. These are research-fixture settings, not commissioned hardware limits. Initial velocity is inherited and decelerated, so the first accepted elbow commands can be 0.42–0.49 rad/s: 0.20 is a desired speed, not a hard initial cap.

26 tests passed (13 parameterized cases on each of two anchors), including transition/ownership, missed/stale updates and selected failure paths. These are not 26 independent safety guarantees. Real publisher leases, relay stop semantics and physical guard behavior remain unverified.

Independent review: the prototype is not a robust control implementation. On lead-guard fallback, actual position-target jumps are 0.03382/0.03731/0.03842 rad, and velocity setpoints can drop by about 0.25 rad/s in one call. These do not preserve the normal reference step/slew contract, although stored finite replay effort estimates remain within the fixture caps. This is command discontinuity, not measured joint speed.
  • Fallback validates a pose and estimated effort but ignores a failed hold write. Ordinary writer exceptions do not demonstrate a latched safe stop.
  • Velocity/effort fields lack complete non-finite rejection; a NaN comparison can bypass the effort check in the offline prototype. No such value was observed in the finite nominal dataset.
  • Re-arming after STOP is not proven to restore/verify actuator mode. Passing hold re-arm tests does not establish that separate contract.
  • A reported synthetic buffer-to-plant equality field is a self-comparison, not independent transport verification. It is not used as delivery evidence here.

These gaps were retained transparently rather than silently patched after observing the results. The positive nominal actuator calculation remains valid under its assumptions, but a future implementation must specify safe handoff/fallback, finite-input checks, acknowledged local writes and supervisory re-arm/lease behavior before further deployment-oriented tests.

3. Synthetic actuator check: the expected stiffness benefit

A four-joint plant used constant model-derived inertia, unchanged XML damping/armature, ideal gravity compensation and stationary remaining coordinates. It compared the two command laws at both sideways anchors under predeclared +1 and −1 Nm disturbances: eight cells, not a fitted real-actuator model.

The approximately 88.74% reduction matches the simple stiffness prediction, 1/16.9154 versus 1/150 rad per Nm. All 301 recovery updates per synthetic cell were accepted; peak lead stayed below 0.023 rad and peak estimated effort below 4.6 Nm. This is useful mechanism consistency across both load signs and arm anchors, not evidence of 88.74% better robot balance.

Movement/fade, whole-body recovery, endpoint tracking, friction/transport realism and the new ALMI policy were not validated by this plant. No physical candidate passes the original selection screen because the required full-body comparison is unavailable.

What would count as a general improvement?

  • The same command law, parameters, timing and constraints must be used across mirrored arms and trajectory families; no branch on “FAME” or a target ID.
  • Requested, buffered and measured action must agree with the proposed mechanism; local publication flags are not motor acknowledgements.
  • Any recovery-only treatment must leave movement/fade unchanged. Pre-treatment differences are initial-state/execution variability, not an improvement caused by recovery.
  • Passing on FAME is not proof for every lower policy. The new ALMI checkpoint needs its own fixed-policy baseline and later matched candidate comparison.
  • Whole-body stability and extraction-contact success remain separate from joint return or optimizer residual. Preserve failed/incomplete comparisons and uncertainty.

Recommended next steps

  1. Continue the generic recovery mechanism offline. A persistent position reference addresses an actuator-interface weakness rather than exploiting a policy. First correct and test the prototype's explicit fault-contract gaps, including feasible handoff/fallback transitions and finite/write/mode checks. Then validate whole-body response and endpoint tracking on a valid baseline. Better elbow return alone is not the goal.
  2. For movement, replace the success proxy before selecting a redesign. Use observed task-added orientation/rate and manipulation tracking as outcome checks, not lower arm-only or full-CAM norm. Repair/validate velocity and angular-rate consistency before selecting momentum-rate penalties or constraints. A physically motivated reaction-rate/shoulder-wrench or response-aware formulation is a future hypothesis; the current logs do not identify ground reaction or contact feasibility. No full-body MPC rewrite is justified yet.
  3. Collect the separate new-ALMI baseline when it is commissioned. The appended operator block mirrors the four FAME cases and repeats, with the same unchanged upper controller. A checkpoint hash is saved and checked before each launch. Do not pool it with old ALMI runs or alter controller gains to make one policy look better. The upcoming comparison can test whether the mechanism persists, not guarantee that every policy benefits.

The good news is a reproducible mechanism and a clearer test contract: mirrored command/position plateaus, active feasible requests, and a simple stiffness prediction that survives both load signs in the isolated plant. The unresolved part is useful full-body behavior. Keep that distinction explicit rather than presenting a successful actuator fixture as a working humanoid controller.
