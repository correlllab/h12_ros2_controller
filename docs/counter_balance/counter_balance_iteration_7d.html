Iteration 7d / prospective simulation and transfer test

Select for base sway, not arm cancellation

Keep the measured reach unchanged. Test the action and the prediction separately. A per-trial hindsight winner is neither a deployable policy nor evidence of a balance gain.

1. Freeze the comparator

On a fixed trajectory and lower policy compare Frame, unchanged Counter (C0), and half feedforward (FF-half). The primary score is time-weighted RMS change in scalar pelvis tilt relative to move onset, in degrees. Full-record peak tilt change, terminal tilt and peak torso angular speed are separate guards. Wrist error is a task check, not the ranking metric.

November 23 confirmed the policy split: FF-half reduced left-overhang sway relative to C0 on both FAME visits but did not reliably beat Frame. On ALMI Stand, C0 had the lowest move sway twice and FF-half the lowest peak twice. Right-diagonal Counter retained a sway advantage where measured. The second FAME diagonal C0 was missing; do not manufacture a balanced triplet.

2. Test prediction without using hardware outcomes

Use independent, pre-collection candidate rollouts of the exact bank trajectory, clock and lower-policy identity. Require all three candidates and complete runs. Choose before observing the evaluation trial; never use its counter-arm commands, state trajectory or outcomes as forecasts. Compare against always-Frame, C0 and FF-half, and count abstentions. If evidence is missing or the model cannot rank the measured actions, do not switch on hardware.

First test a conservative selector on the November 22 simulation versus the November 23 hardware blocks. Require a reproducible move-sway reduction over Frame and no increase in peak tilt, peak torso rate or final tilt change; Frame is the fallback. This is pre-reach selection from cached simulation forecasts, not online MPC. Nikolaus's FAME/MuJoCo planner is a simulation upper-bound comparator, not a transferable source of hardware commands.

3. Test one new action

The 7c frozen-state experiment lowered modeled perpendicular counter-arm CoM velocity by 22–23%. In a simulation-only C0 variant, penalize counter-arm horizontal CoM velocity perpendicular to the planned arm disturbance. Retain C0 feedback, posture, bounds and finalizer; suppress ill-conditioned directions near zero velocity. Compare against C0, Frame and FF-half to distinguish axis selectivity from attenuation. Do not combine with a new return law or the yaw row.

Start with the left sideways reach and the right diagonal sentinel. Hold out the mirrored right sideways reach, then test the other saved trajectories in both banks without changing weights. Record the actual measured excursion; if it is not matched, the experiment cannot attribute a sway change uniquely to direction.

First mechanism check: direction versus amplitude

On all seven recorded November 23 C0 trials we reproduced the requested bounded solve and added the cross-axis row on the identical state. As a scalar control, rescale the original C0 joint velocity to exactly the new action's norm without changing direction; exclude intervals where that control violates the original bounds. On the four left overhang traces, this retains 83–89% of the move interval. Compared with that equal-speed control, perpendicular model CoM velocity RMS falls 22% and the parallel residual increases 0.08–0.09 mm/s. This shows a change in arm direction that scaling alone does not reproduce. It does not measure body sway, closed-loop excursion, or safety on hardware. The three diagonal C0 traces are also reported; no fourth trace was imputed.

4. Promotion gate

The first two cold left-overhang C0/cross-axis comparisons changed move sway from 0.237 to 0.260° and from 0.236 to 0.211°. Opposite signs and nearly identical means argue against promotion, even though the simulated actuator received the new command and the frozen model residual decreased.

The mirrored right sideways pair changed from 0.509 to 0.486°, and the right diagonal from 0.786 to 0.753°. Each is only one cold FAME pair, not a replicated improvement over Frame or FF-half. All eight runs were operationally valid; all failed the timed wrist gate. Protocol, raw-data location and reproduction commands.

Selector result: no counter action was selected on any of the 20 catalog targets. Seventeen had no independent forecasts, two had only one run per action, and on the repeated left target neither candidate beat Frame's sway envelope. Frame fallback is not a validated win. The raw, unguarded FAME ranking matched the lowest mean hardware sway on one of the two observed targets.
