Timing improved. Robust balance is not established.

Frame versus unchanged Counter, with repeated overhang cases. The scheduler now achieves similar cadence for both controllers, but Counter still worsens timed tracking and has important case-dependent balance regressions.

12 recordings, 6 explicit pairs, 4,035 samples. No hardware operation, controller change or safety-threshold relaxation was performed for this analysis.

Decision

Proceed with bounded offline 7b work, not a robust-deployment or extraction-contact claim. Keep the existing Frame and Counter baselines. Include the two small onset warnings with flags. Investigate the separate late-hold pause and repeat the newly regressing ALMI diagonal case before interpreting a controller redesign as safe or generally better.

Confirmed improvement

Ordinary Frame/Counter application cadence is now close to the same 33.33 ms budget. The old sustained Counter cadence penalty is no longer present, although Counter computation still takes longer.

Confirmed remaining penalty

Executed-clock timed and joint tracking are worse in 6/6 pairs; spatial RMS is worse in 5/6. FAME sideways regressions repeat in both controller orders.

Useful but local behavior

ALMI inner-forward Counter suppresses added movement tilt in both repeats, but retains the prepared lean. It does not restore the earlier standing orientation.

ALMI inner-forward strongly suppresses additional movement tilt, but remains far from the earlier standing reference. Do not summarize this as either “all balancing failed” or “directional benefit is preserved.”

Corrected timed tracking is worse with Counter in all six pairs; frozen spatial RMS is worse in five of six. Terminal standing-relative drift is also higher in all six official pair reductions, but the B4 Frame tail is sparse. The clean repeat R4 and a continuous pre-gap B4 window support the direction of that result without filling the missing interval.

Interpretation of the ALMI inner-forward result

B4/R4 begin around 5.2–5.6° from standing. Counter reduces move tilt RMS from 0.200/0.166° to 0.034/0.031°, respectively. This is a real observed reduction in added tilt, not a return to upright. In the continuous B4 window [T+7,T+9], standing-relative mean drift is 4.232° Frame versus 5.188° Counter, while onset-relative orientation is 1.061° versus 0.473°. Counter holds closer to its prepared orientation; Frame moves farther toward the earlier standing orientation. Which is desirable for extraction depends on the calibrated task/fixture reference, not the classifier alone.

Onset warnings and timing

Include the two small onset-warning runs, but preserve their flags. B3 Counter and R3 Counter start recording at 53.841 and 51.991 ms, respectively, exceeding the 50 ms rule by 3.841 and 1.991 ms. Both auto-stop normally and retain the movement and long hold. Excluding them would remove both FAME sideways pairs, not merely a random 2/12 sample.

The execution epoch is set after validation; synchronous recorder metadata/event work then occurs before the first task observation. The start-authorized events for the warned runs are already at epoch +51.392 and +49.622 ms. This is evidence of startup bookkeeping/scheduling contribution, not proof of unstable networking.

At those first observations, the 1.2 s quintic reference has advanced only 0.084%/0.076% of its path, or 0.253/0.228 mm. That supports their use for flagged motion analysis, but it does not bound unobserved physical movement. Common-window resampling preserves the tracking and tilt conclusions; strict exclusion would erase the FAME sideways case entirely.

Separate issue: B4 Frame has a late gap. Observation and command-start gaps are approximately 888.57 and 888.72 ms between elapsed 10.304 and 11.192 s, with 25 skipped slots. Its original completeness flag passes because the continuity rule mainly covers the required movement window. Do not treat that flag as proof of uninterrupted terminal hold, or interpolate across this gap and call the result measured.

Onset coverage, runtime state freshness, publication and physical safety are distinct checks. No state-age guard or safety threshold has been relaxed. The current state reader timestamps its on-demand latest-sample take, not proven device acquisition or network arrival; small recorded observation age does not establish small transport latency. Publication/actuator receipt remain unknown.

All 2,028 Counter rows had accepted nonzero actions. Applied velocity matches the local buffer exactly; counter position follows measured position plus one nominal step to within 3×10−8 rad. There are zero solver failures/retries, invalid candidates, collision backtracks or reconstructed limit violations. The inactive-counter problem seen in old FAME simulation is not present in this campaign.

Inner-forward has no active motion bounds yet a repeatable requested-to-measured gap. Diagonal is frequently bound-limited. More importantly, B2 has a better nominal residual than B1 and similar counter realization, yet much worse base response. The allocator does not predict lower-policy/contact/base dynamics. Delivering its current optimum more strongly is not automatically a solution.

Moving-arm velocity feedforward is clipped in 12/40 move rows of each Counter diagonal run, by up to about 0.167 rad/s, but not materially in the overhang runs. Position targets continue advancing. Command bounds are not guaranteed physical-state bounds. No counter feedforward torque clipping is seen; total downstream motor torque saturation is not ruled out.

A reproducible recovery plateau

After the 0.5 s fade, balancing costs are zero but posture return remains active. During [T+7,T+9], FAME sideways elbow offsets remain about 0.166 rad in both repeats despite requested return velocity near −0.166 rad/s and measured velocity near −0.008/−0.002 rad/s. ALMI inner-forward offsets remain −0.077 rad with commands around +0.077 rad/s but measured motion only +0.005/+0.004 rad/s.

Current command: q_cmd = q_measured + dt * u; dq_cmd = u
Approximate motor PD: tau_PD = (Kp * dt + Kd) * u - Kd * dq_measured
Postfade: u ≈ -(q_measured - q_reference) / 1.005

Under that local PD interpretation, effective elbow return stiffness is about 16.92 Nm/rad, not its nominal position gain of 150 Nm/rad. This explains why the interface is not equivalent to following a persistent position reference. It does not identify actual friction, gravity error, relay behavior or motor torque. A bounded recovery-reference experiment is justified; a blanket gain increase is not.

Recovery is only one problem. B2 Counter reaches 3.589° onset-relative orientation and 3.561° gravity-direction change before motion ends. Its global onset excursion peaks during fade at T+0.384 s. B3/R3 also deteriorate during movement, then peak near T+0.68/0.71 s. A postfade-only change cannot repair these earlier regressions.

What this cannot establish

  • No measured world base translation, fixture-relative peg motion, contact force or extraction success is inferred from FK and IMU data.
  • A small repeated panel supports a targeted next experiment, not a certified robust operating envelope.
  • A local command-buffer timestamp does not prove network transmission or actuator receipt.
